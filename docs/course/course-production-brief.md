## Learning format

Proposed 32 lessons, each approximately 4–6 minutes, followed by three original application questions with explanations. Each lesson starts with a client situation, explains one decision or comparison, shows a visual example, and ends with a new situation the learner must solve. State-law lessons should receive proportionally more practice time. Total planned video time is approximately 2–3 hours; practice and review add study time.

Use realistic fictional clients for conversations and decisions. Use clean animated timelines, ownership arrows, comparison charts, and money-flow diagrams for rules and calculations. Generate short scenario footage in Higgsfield; build precise labels and numbers in the edit. Keep teaching narration separately editable so a legal or tax correction does not require regenerating the entire scene.

## Practice and mastery

- Diagnostic: original questions sampling every exam section; give a topic report.
- Lesson drills: three questions with plausible distractors, an explanation for every option, and a link to the exact lesson segment.
- Review queue: revisit misses after a delay using a new scenario, not just repeated answer wording.
- Mock exams: independent forms matching the published section counts and exam timing; clearly identify all questions as practice content.
- Proposed internal readiness target: at least 85% on two fresh mixed mocks and no topic below 80%, followed by review of remaining misses. This is a course target, not a validated prediction or a guarantee of passing.
- Do not market a guaranteed pass or official approval. Describe the product as exam preparation with targeted practice and progress tracking.
