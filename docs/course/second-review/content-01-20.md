## Limits and release interpretation

Sources were accessed on the review date. The linked Prometric outline uses an older filename; it was treated as the currently available linked outline, not relabeled as a newly issued2026 document. Conceptual NAIC material is older and was used only for stable definitions. Current legal conclusions use Maryland statutes/COMAR and current agency materials. This check did not establish that no2026 session law can take effect later; future October1 changes must be distinguished from rules effective September13, not silently incorporated as current law.

All assigned questions were read for answer correctness and ambiguity. This was not a psychometric validation of exam difficulty. Several deliberately easy distractors are suitable for formative checks but those checks alone do not establish readiness for unseen exam questions. No photographed exam items were used as authority.

The ordinary exam “foreign/alien” taxonomy is suitable in Part4. Maryland §1–101 also uses “foreign” as a broader statutory term that can include alien insurers unless context indicates otherwise. That nuance is not a reason to mark the current Virginia-example question wrong.

Final release still depends on the root review's actual audiovisual checks, learner-flow regression and coordinator judgment. The caption matches recorded here are text comparisons; they do not prove that an audio generator omitted no word, repeated no syllable, or produced no abrupt edit.
