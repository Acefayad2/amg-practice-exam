## Proposed question

Luis owns an in-force individual life policy issued October 2, 2026 and governed by Maryland law. Its payable death benefit is **$250,000**. Rosa is the sole primary beneficiary. His adult daughter Maya is the sole contingent beneficiary and receives the full benefit if Rosa is treated as having died before Luis.

Luis dies on **October 15, 2026 at 9:00 a.m.** Rosa dies on **October 17 at 9:00 a.m.**, exactly **48 hours later**. These times are established by clear and convincing evidence. Maya is still alive on November 1 and is otherwise eligible.

The policy has no applicable simultaneous-death/common-disaster language, no different survival period, and no express waiver of a specified survival period. Assume no other statutory exception applies, no federal rule displaces Maryland law, and there is no assignment, competing claim or other payment restriction.

**Who receives the $250,000 under these facts?**

A. Rosa’s estate, because any proven survival after Luis is sufficient.

B. Maya, because Rosa did not satisfy the applicable survival requirement.

C. Maya and Rosa’s estate, with $125,000 each because two beneficiaries are named.

D. Luis’s estate, because Maryland always requires a primary beneficiary to survive for 30 days.

## Answer and explanations

**Correct answer: B — Maya receives $250,000.** Forty-eight hours is less than 120 hours (five days). With the stated exceptions excluded, Rosa is treated as predeceasing Luis for this beneficiary provision. The stipulated contingent designation therefore gives Maya the benefit. [Chapter 509, new Courts and Judicial Proceedings §10-803, printed page 4](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2026RS/Chapters_noln/CH_509_hb0596t.pdf#page=4)

- **A:** Proof of 48-hour survival does not meet this default.
- **B:** Correctly combines the survival rule with the stated contingent designation.
- **C:** Primary and contingent designations in this example are successive, not equal shares.
- **D:** Thirty days is not the default taught here; an express different policy period would require separate analysis. [§10-806(3), printed page 6](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2026RS/Chapters_noln/CH_509_hb0596t.pdf#page=6)
