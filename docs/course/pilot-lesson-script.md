## 2. First distinction — narrated timeline, approximately 35 seconds

Visual: A household icon above a horizontal line. A teal bar labeled “20-year term” ends at a clear endpoint. Beneath it, place a separate card reading “No cash value.” Use typography in the edit.

Narration: “Start with two questions: how long is the protection designed to last, and does the policy build cash value? Term insurance covers a specified period. In this example, the policy lasts twenty years. If the insured dies while covered, the policy pays its death benefit under the contract. Ordinary term insurance does not build cash value. Reaching the end of the term does not, by itself, create a payout.”

## 3. Whole-life comparison — narrated diagram, approximately 35 seconds

Visual: Second row labeled “Traditional whole life.” A long coverage bar and a separate schematic cash-value line. Add “Illustration only — no dollar projection.” No price estimates or invented investment returns.

Narration: “Traditional whole life is designed for permanent protection and includes guaranteed cash value under its terms. Required premiums and other contract conditions still matter. Its premium structure differs from term, and comparable term coverage generally starts with a lower premium. For this lesson, remember the core contrast: a specified protection period with no cash value, versus permanent protection with contractual cash value.”

## 4. Apply the distinction — approximately 25 seconds

Visual: Two anonymous client cards. A: “Temporary income-protection need.” B: “Permanent protection and guaranteed cash value.” Reveal the product match only after a pause.

Narration: “One client asks for temporary protection. Another specifically asks for permanent coverage and guaranteed cash value. Which product description fits each request? Pause and make the match. The first description points to term; the second points to traditional whole life. This identifies product features. A real recommendation still requires a fuller review of the client's needs and budget.”

## 5. Question one — approximately 35 seconds with pause

On-screen question: “A policy covers a stated number of years and has no cash value. Which feature identifies it most directly?”

A. Permanent protection
B. A specified coverage period
C. Guaranteed annual dividends
D. Flexible investment subaccounts

Narration: “Choose your answer before continuing.”

Pause: 8 seconds, with a visible pause-to-think instruction.

Reveal: B.

Explanation: “The stated coverage period is the clue. Permanent protection describes a different design. Dividends are not guaranteed. Investment subaccounts are not a feature of ordinary term insurance.”

## 6. Question two — approximately 35 seconds with pause

On-screen question: “Which description best matches traditional whole life?”

A. Temporary protection without cash value
B. Permanent protection with contractual guaranteed cash value
C. A guaranteed stock-market return
D. Coverage that continues regardless of required payments

Pause: 8 seconds.

Reveal: B.

Explanation: “Whole life combines permanent protection and cash value under the contract. A describes term. C confuses policy guarantees with investment-market performance. D ignores the obligations and conditions that keep coverage in force.”

## 7. Transfer question and recap — approximately 45 seconds

On-screen question: “A learner says: ‘My ordinary term policy has lasted ten years, so it must now have a cash value.’ What is the mistake?”

Pause: 8 seconds.

Narration: “Time alone does not turn term insurance into a cash-value policy. The contract's product design determines its features. Some term contracts offer conversion rights, which we cover next, but conversion is a separate contractual option. Explain today's comparison out loud: term has a defined coverage period and no cash value; traditional whole life is designed for permanent protection with guaranteed cash value under its terms. Then try a new question without looking at the chart.”
