01 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

A flexible payment does not remove the monthly costs

Elena owns universal life coverage and wants to reduce her payments while changing jobs. Universal life separates premium funding, interest credits, and policy deductions. The owner may have flexibility within the contract, but insurance costs and administrative charges continue. A planned premium is not automatically the amount guaranteed to sustain coverage forever. If payments and earnings do not support the deductions, the available value can decline. Without an applicable protection provision, insufficient funding can lead to lapse after the required process. First read the policy's funding conditions and current statement, then discuss the effect of the proposed change.

02 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Trace a simplified account for one month

Use a deliberately simplified teaching account. It begins with one thousand dollars. A two-hundred-dollar payment adds one hundred ninety dollars after an assumed ten-dollar premium charge. Assume twenty dollars of interest is credited and one hundred thirty dollars of insurance and other charges are deducted. The ending value is one thousand eighty dollars. Now remove the payment while keeping the other assumptions fixed. The ending value becomes eight hundred ninety dollars. This example illustrates separate flows, not a real carrier's calculation order or guaranteed monthly return. Missing a payment does not make the insurance charges disappear.

03 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Current assumptions and guarantees are different

Interest-sensitive and current-assumption designs respond to the pricing and crediting terms stated in the contract. An indeterminate-premium whole life design may change its current premium within a guaranteed maximum. Universal life instead presents flexible funding and separately identified charges and credits. Do not treat those structures as identical. The insurer's current credited rate or illustrated performance may differ from the contractual minimum. Rising insurance costs or lower earnings can affect the funding needed. A no-lapse guarantee, when included, has its own required payment pattern, period, and conditions. The word guarantee is useful only when you identify what is guaranteed and what must be done.

04 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Indexed crediting uses a contract formula

Indexed life links a credited-interest calculation to a named external index under the policy's rules. An indexed universal life policy does not directly invest the owner's policy value in that stock index. A participation rate determines the share of the stated index change used. A cap limits the credited rate, while a spread can reduce the rate under a different stated formula. A floor provides the stated minimum for that crediting calculation. Methods and available rates differ by contract and strategy. Do not present the index's headline return as the policy's actual return or assume that stock dividends are included.

05 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Apply the stated formula before discussing results

Assume a teaching strategy uses an eight percent positive index change, seventy-five percent participation, a five percent cap, and a zero percent floor, with no spread. Participation produces six percent. The cap reduces the credited rate to five percent. Under the same strategy, a negative index change would produce zero percent interest because of the floor. These are illustrative assumptions, not currently offered rates. Policy charges still apply. Zero index interest can accompany a falling policy value after deductions. A crediting floor does not promise that the owner's net cash value can never decline.

06 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Review the policy instead of relying on a headline

Elena should review the death-benefit option, current account and surrender values, deductions, premium history, and any guarantee's status. Some universal designs offer a level benefit, while others add the account value to a stated amount. Use the actual option and policy rules; do not assume every policy pays both amounts. Benefit increases may require new evidence of insurability. Request an updated in-force illustration using appropriate assumptions when reviewing future funding. Explain which figures are guaranteed and which are projections. A sound review connects the client's continuing protection need to an affordable funding plan and the contract's actual conditions.

APPLY / IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Go one step deeper.

The monthly arithmetic isolates each flow

The assumed premium charge leaves $190 of the $200 payment. Then $1,000 + $190 + $20 − $130 = $1,080. Without that payment, $1,000 + $20 − $130 = $890. Real contracts specify their own deductions, timing, rates and calculation order.

Example: If a question changes the expense assumption, recalculate from the new facts rather than remembering $1,080.

A no-lapse guarantee has its own test

Some policies can retain the stated death protection despite a low account value if the guarantee’s actual conditions remain satisfied. The period, timely funding, loans, withdrawals and policy changes can matter. Read the contract rather than assuming all universal policies have the same guarantee.

Example: Paying less may affect a guarantee even when there is still account value.

Separate indexed crediting from indexed benefit changes

The narrated example concerns an indexed interest strategy. Other indexed life designs may change coverage according to an index or a specified rider. Identify which policy element is linked to the index before applying a formula.

Example: A cost-of-living benefit increase is not automatically an index-crediting deposit into cash value.

A spread example must state its formula

If a teaching strategy expressly credits a 7% positive index change minus a 2-percentage-point spread, with a 0% floor and no cap, the resulting credited rate is 5% before policy deductions. Do not insert participation, a cap or a different order unless stated.

Example: 7% minus 2 percentage points is 5%; multiplying 7% by 98% answers a different calculation.

Read the death-benefit option

A common level option keeps a stated total death benefit, subject to contract and tax-law requirements. A common increasing option adds account value to the stated amount. Names such as Option A and Option B depend on the contract. Loans and other adjustments can change the actual payment.

Example: With explicit no-adjustment assumptions, a $200,000 stated amount plus $30,000 account value produces $230,000 under an option that expressly adds them. A level $200,000 option has a different starting calculation.
