01 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Start with the contract and the claim

Aisha contacts the insurer after her husband's death. The producer helps her locate the policy, identify the beneficiary designation and obtain the required claim instructions. The insurer evaluates the claim under the contract and applicable law. Maryland's form-review process generally requires covered life policy forms to be filed and approved, subject to stated exceptions. That oversight does not allow a producer to rewrite a benefit at claim time. Explain the actual provisions, help the family provide the needed information and avoid promising an outcome before the facts are reviewed.

02 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

A denial needs a sound basis

An insurer cannot refuse a claim for an arbitrary reason, misrepresent a relevant policy provision or quietly rely on an altered application. Maryland also requires a prompt, reasonable explanation on request for a claim denial. Another section addresses specified unfair practices committed often enough to indicate a general business practice. That includes repeated failures to investigate reasonably or respond promptly. Do not conclude that an isolated prohibited act becomes acceptable because there is no pattern. The two sections address overlapping conduct with different requirements. Identify the facts and the applicable rule.

03 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Interest has its own timing rule

For life policies issued in Maryland, the basic interest rule runs from the insured's death until payment. The insurer need not pay interest if it pays the proceeds within thirty days after death. If proof of death arrives more than one hundred eighty days after death, interest instead runs from submission of that proof until payment. The rate is at least the rate payable on death proceeds left on deposit with the insurer. Maryland has corresponding individual and group life provisions. Keep this interest calculation separate from whether the underlying death benefit is payable.

04 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

If the insurer becomes insolvent

Now suppose the insurer fails financially. Maryland's Life and Health Insurance Guaranty Corporation exists to protect eligible people against specified failures of member insurers, within legal limits. It is a private, nonprofit, nonstock corporation, not a state department. Member insurers fund its work through assessments. Depending on the circumstances, protection may involve continuing covered obligations or arranging a transfer to another insurer. Owners should follow the receiver's instructions and keep required premiums current to maintain coverage. Insolvency does not automatically make a policy free or turn every promise into a state debt.

05 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Understand the limits before applying them

The Maryland life limits include up to three hundred thousand dollars in death benefits, with no more than one hundred thousand dollars in net cash surrender or withdrawal values. The annuity limit is two hundred fifty thousand dollars in present value benefits. These are statutory ceilings, not additional benefits added to the policy. Contractual obligations, eligibility, exclusions and the aggregate limit still matter. Multiple policies on one life do not multiply the protection. Investment risk borne by a policyholder and nonguaranteed portions are excluded under the stated rule. Check the complete contract and the law.

06 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Protection is not a sales incentive

A producer tells Elena to ignore an insurer's financial condition because guaranty protection makes the purchase risk free. That is wrong. Maryland prohibits using this protection in the sale of insurance. The law separately requires a written notice for a delivered contract that exceeds the stated limits or is outside the coverage, explaining those limits and the corporation's private status. A required disclosure is different from a sales inducement. Explain the insurer, its actual promises and the product's risks accurately. Guaranty protection never replaces a careful, suitable coverage decision.

APPLY / IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Go one step deeper.

Eligibility is more than a policy’s purchase location

For ordinary covered contracts, §9–403 generally looks to a Maryland-resident owner or certificate holder. Qualifying beneficiaries, assignees and payees are protected regardless of their residence. Special nonresident rules and coordination with other states prevent duplicate protection. Structured settlement annuities have separate eligibility provisions.

Example: A beneficiary living elsewhere is not automatically excluded when the covered owner meets the Maryland conditions.

The ceilings do not create a second payout

§9–407 limits liability to the lesser of the covered contractual obligation or the statutory amount, with the stated exclusions and aggregate rules. For the life and annuity benefits taught here, the ordinary aggregate is $300,000 with respect to one life; the life cash-value limit is a sublimit.

Example: Two covered life policies on one life, each with a $250,000 death obligation at the failed insurer, do not create a $600,000 guaranty ceiling. The life ceiling is $300,000, subject to the full rules.

Several excluded obligations require care

§9–403 excludes the portion for which the owner bears investment risk, specified nonguaranteed portions, dividends, certain self-funded arrangements and specified unlicensed-at-issue contracts. A variable contract’s qualifying general-account guarantee is a separate question from the owner’s market loss.

Example: A decline in separate-account investments does not become a covered guaranteed return merely because the insurer is a member.

Apply the late-proof interest exception

Sections 16–109 and 17–102 use the same basic dates taught in the video. Proof submitted after 180 days shifts the interest starting point to proof submission. Do not confuse the thirty-day interest exception with permission to delay or mishandle a claim.

Example: Proof arrives 200 days after death and proceeds are paid 20 days later. Under the stated exception, interest runs for the period after proof submission, not all 220 days.

Form approval has a defined process

For forms governed by §12–203, the ordinary filing period is at least sixty days before delivery; approval waives the unexpired period. The Commissioner can extend it up to thirty additional days with timely notice, and the statute addresses deemed approval. Exceptions, exemptions and permitted filing procedures must be considered.

Example: An insurer’s approved form is not permission for a producer to invent an unfiled benefit on a sales flyer.
