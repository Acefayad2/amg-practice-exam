01 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Who is acting for whom?

Aisha is a licensed life producer. Marcus asks whether she can promise that every application will be approved. She cannot. A producer's role and authority have limits. In a typical insurer agency relationship, the insurer is the principal and the producer is its agent. The principal authorizes the agent to perform specified acts on its behalf. The producer may have duties to applicants and insureds as well. The word agent identifies a relationship; it does not mean permission to rewrite a policy or ignore underwriting.

02 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Express authority

Express authority is specifically granted, often in the producer's written agreement. For example, an insurer may authorize Aisha to solicit applications and collect an initial premium using its approved receipt. She should read what the grant permits and what it excludes. A life producer ordinarily does not have unrestricted power to bind coverage or waive application requirements. If Marcus asks for a guarantee outside her authority, the correct response is to explain the limits and obtain the insurer's decision, not invent an approval.

03 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Implied authority

Implied authority includes actions reasonably necessary to carry out the express grant. If Aisha is authorized to solicit and submit applications, explaining how to complete the approved form may be incidental to that work. Implied authority is not a way to bypass an express restriction. Changing a premium rate, deleting an exclusion, or promising an underwriting result is not justified merely because it would help make a sale. Always connect the incidental action to the task the principal actually authorized.

04 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Apparent authority

Apparent authority concerns what a third party reasonably believes because of the principal's words or conduct. Suppose the insurer presents a person to customers as authorized to perform a particular task, and a customer reasonably relies on that presentation. That can create an apparent-authority issue even if a private instruction is narrower. The producer's unsupported statement, I can do anything, is not enough by itself. Look for conduct attributable to the principal and reasonable reliance. Whether an insurer is legally bound depends on the actual facts and applicable law.

05 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Captive and independent relationships

A captive or exclusive producer generally represents one insurer or affiliated group. An independent producer may work with several insurers. Neither label replaces licensing, appointment or contract requirements. In Maryland, written appointment documentation and the insurer's producer register have their own rules. A business entity's relationship and an individual's authority should not be assumed identical. For this lesson, remember that a producer's contract governs the working relationship, while a state license and an insurer appointment answer separate regulatory and authorization questions.

06 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Put the client’s file first

Aisha's useful work is accurate fact gathering, honest explanation, careful handling of premiums and private information, and prompt transmission of the application. She must not forge a signature, conceal a material answer, or describe a nonguaranteed value as guaranteed. If she lacks an answer, she should verify it. When you classify authority on an exam, ask where it came from: a direct grant, a necessary incidental action, or the principal's appearance to the customer. Then choose the action that remains within that authority.

APPLY / IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Go one step deeper.

Apparent authority is not self-created

An agent cannot establish unlimited authority merely by claiming to have it. In a legal analysis, identify the principal’s manifestations, the third party’s reasonable belief, and the particular transaction. Do not presume that every customer expectation binds an insurer.

Example: An insurer’s own notice directs customers to a person for a particular transaction. That is different from a person privately claiming authority the insurer never suggested.
