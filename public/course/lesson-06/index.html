01 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

From application to agreement

Marcus signs an application and pays an initial premium. Has he automatically received every benefit he requested? Not necessarily. A valid contract needs agreement, consideration, competent parties and a legal purpose. The application commonly constitutes an offer, and the insurer decides whether to accept under its rules. If it offers different coverage or a higher premium, that may be a counteroffer requiring acceptance. The precise effective date also depends on the application, receipt and policy conditions. A signature or payment alone is not a universal coverage rule.

02 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

What each side gives

Consideration is the value exchanged for the contractual promise. The applicant supplies the premium and application statements; the insurer supplies its promise to pay according to the contract. Competent parties must have the legal capacity to contract. A lawful purpose means the arrangement cannot be built around an illegal objective. Life insurance on another person also raises insurable-interest and consent requirements. These ideas are related but different. A perfectly completed form cannot make an illegal betting arrangement valid merely because someone paid a premium.

03 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Adhesion and an unequal exchange

Insurance is commonly described as a contract of adhesion because the insurer prepares the standard terms and the applicant generally accepts or rejects them. That does not mean a clear exclusion disappears. We will study interpretation separately. The contract is also aleatory: the value exchanged depends on an uncertain event and may be unequal. Marcus could pay a relatively small amount before a covered death produces a large benefit. Another insured may pay premiums for many years without a death claim during a term policy's coverage period.

04 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Unilateral and conditional

A life policy is usually described as unilateral because the insurer makes the enforceable promise to pay covered benefits. The owner can generally stop paying future premiums, subject to policy consequences such as lapse or use of available values. This does not mean the policy has no conditions. Coverage may depend on required premiums, truthful application information and other valid terms. Unilateral identifies the contractual promise. Conditional reminds you that the promise operates under specified requirements. Do not mistake either word for an unconditional promise to pay every claim.

Unilateral

Insurer makes the benefit promise

Conditional

Specified requirements still apply

05 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

A stated benefit, not every actual expense

Indemnity generally aims to compensate an actual covered financial loss without creating a profit from that loss. Life insurance ordinarily pays a stated contractual death benefit rather than measuring the person's economic worth after death. The family does not submit every future household expense to set that amount. The personal-contract concept also needs care: many insurance contracts closely connect coverage to a particular insured risk, but life policies generally permit assignment of ownership rights under their terms and applicable law. Assignment does not change which person's life is insured.

Indemnity

Compensation for a measured covered loss

Life death benefit

Amount determined by the contract

Assignment

Transfers rights; not the insured life

06 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Read the facts in order

Suppose an applicant requests one rate, but the insurer offers a higher rate based on underwriting. Identify the counteroffer and the applicant's response before assuming a contract on the original terms. Then check the consideration, legal capacity and lawful purpose. Finally, apply the policy's conditions. The exam may ask only for a characteristic, such as aleatory or adhesion, but a real file can involve several at once. Learn the reason each term fits, so a changed scenario does not depend on memorized wording.

APPLY / IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Go one step deeper.

Do not confuse legal capacity with underwriting eligibility

Capacity concerns legal ability to enter a contract. Underwriting concerns whether and on what terms an insurer will accept a risk. An adult can have capacity but receive a rated offer; special rules may permit certain minors to contract for life coverage.

Example: A higher premium caused by risk classification is not proof that the applicant lacks legal capacity.

Assignment does not insure a different person

An assignment transfers specified policy rights. A collateral assignment gives a creditor a limited security interest; an absolute assignment transfers ownership rights more broadly. Neither simply substitutes a new insured life. Later lessons address consent, beneficiaries and tax consequences.

Example: Marcus assigns policy rights as collateral for a loan. Marcus remains the insured.
