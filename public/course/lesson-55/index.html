01 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

The word move is not a complete instruction

David changes jobs and asks to move retirement money. First identify the arrangement sending the funds, the arrangement receiving them and whether the money is pretax, after-tax or Roth. A properly completed eligible rollover can preserve tax deferral, but a pretax-to-Roth conversion generally recognizes the previously untaxed amount as income. A receiving employer plan is not required to accept every rollover. Also confirm whether David is entitled to a distribution under the sending plan. Wanting a different investment does not by itself create a withdrawal right. The correct process starts with eligible accounts and an eligible transaction, before anyone requests a check.

02 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

A direct transfer keeps payment between the institutions

If Rosa moves a traditional individual retirement account directly from one trustee to another, the trustee-to-trustee transfer is not an owner-received sixty-day rollover. It is not restricted by the individual-account one-rollover-per-year rule. For an eligible employer-plan distribution, a direct rollover sends payment to the receiving retirement arrangement and avoids mandatory twenty percent withholding on that direct payment. A check can still qualify as direct when it is payable to the receiving plan or account for the owner's benefit, even if the owner forwards the check. Distinguish who is named as payee from who physically carries the envelope. Obtain the institutions' instructions and preserve the transaction records.

03 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

A payment to the owner starts a different process

Suppose an eligible retirement distribution instead is paid to David personally. A sixty-day rollover generally requires completing the deposit into an eligible receiving arrangement within sixty days after he receives it. Intending to roll over the money is not enough, and depositing it into an ordinary savings account does not complete a retirement rollover. He may roll over all or an eligible portion. Any taxable amount not properly rolled over generally remains income, and an early-distribution additional tax may apply. Relief from the deadline exists in specified circumstances, but a producer cannot simply promise an extension. Confirm the actual receipt date, eligible destination and applicable exception before treating a late transaction as qualified.

04 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Withholding creates a funding gap for a full rollover

David receives a fifty thousand dollar eligible rollover distribution from a pretax employer plan, paid to him personally. Assume the usual twenty percent withholding applies. Ten thousand dollars goes to withholding and he receives forty thousand dollars. To roll over the entire fifty thousand dollars within the ordinary deadline, he must contribute the forty thousand received plus ten thousand from other funds. If he rolls over only forty thousand, the unrolled ten thousand remains taxable under these assumptions. The withheld amount is a tax payment credited on the return; it does not automatically satisfy the full rollover. Also do not treat withholding as the final tax owed.

05 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

The once-per-year rule is not an account-by-account allowance

An owner generally can complete only one covered sixty-day rollover between individual retirement accounts in a twelve-month period across their applicable accounts. Opening a second account does not create another allowance, and January first does not automatically reset the clock. This restriction does not apply to direct trustee transfers, traditional-to-Roth conversions or eligible rollovers between employer plans and individual accounts. Those transactions still have their own requirements. The twenty percent withholding example also belongs to a taxable eligible employer-plan payment. An ordinary individual retirement account payment to its owner generally has a ten percent withholding default with election choices. Do not import one withholding rule into every type of retirement payment.

06 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Not every distribution is eligible to be rolled over

A required minimum distribution cannot be rolled over. Employer-plan hardship distributions and other specifically excluded payments also are not eligible simply because the owner quickly deposits them elsewhere. Inherited arrangements require additional care: a nonspouse beneficiary does not have the same owner-style sixty-day rollover choice as the original owner or an eligible spouse. A permitted direct transfer must preserve the required inherited-account identity. Before the transfer is requested, check eligibility, destination, payment instructions, tax character and deadlines. A rollover is a tax procedure, not proof that the new product is suitable or less expensive. Compare the actual investments, charges, protections and access rights as a separate decision.

APPLY / IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Go one step deeper.

The 12-month period is measured from receipt

The covered IRA rollover limit is generally measured from the date the owner receives the distribution that is rolled over. It applies across the owner’s applicable traditional, Roth, SEP and SIMPLE arrangements, with separate eligibility restrictions. The exclusions include trustee transfers, conversions and plan-to-IRA, IRA-to-plan and plan-to-plan rollovers.

Example: It is not one rollover per account or one reset each calendar year.

Withholding is neither an extra fee nor the final liability

The video assumes a $50,000 fully taxable eligible employer-plan rollover distribution with ordinary 20% withholding. A full eligible rollover needs all $50,000 deposited, including replacement of withheld funds. The tax return credits the withholding. Taxable amounts left outside the rollover can also be subject to an additional tax if no exception applies.

Example: Ordinary IRA nonperiodic payments generally use a 10% withholding default with election rights, rather than mandatory plan-style 20%.

Eligibility comes before the deadline

Required minimum distributions, employer-plan hardships, certain long-period or life payments, corrective distributions and certain deemed loan distributions are excluded. A qualifying plan-loan offset has its own rollover timing rule; do not group it with every deemed loan distribution. A properly handled rollover does not consume the annual regular IRA contribution dollar limit.

Example: IRS deadline relief does not waive every other condition for a valid rollover.

Inherited and Roth accounts retain their own restrictions

A nonspouse inherited IRA cannot simply be rolled into the beneficiary’s own IRA; permitted direct transfers must retain inherited identity. Spousal options differ. A Roth IRA cannot be rolled into a traditional IRA to erase its identity, and a pretax-to-Roth move generally is taxable conversion income.

Example: Check the IRS rollover chart and receiving arrangement’s acceptance rules for the actual account types.
