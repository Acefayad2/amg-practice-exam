01 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Renewal and education are different clocks

Marcus has earned his license. Keeping it active now requires a calendar, accurate records and timely action. Maryland producer licenses are generally renewed every two years. An individual's expiration is tied to the end of the birth month. Renewal requires the appropriate application, fees and qualifications. Continuing education has an earlier deadline. Do not wait until the last day of the license to begin a course. A missed email is especially dangerous if the producer never updated the address held by the Administration.

02 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

The regular continuing-education rule

For a typical Maryland resident life producer, the regular requirement is twenty-four approved continuing-education hours in the renewal period, including at least three in ethics. Complete the required education no later than fifteen days before license expiration. Hours must satisfy the applicable licensed-line requirements. Special statutory reductions, exceptions and approved waivers exist, so do not present the regular rule as universal. A nonresident can satisfy Maryland's continuing-education requirement through qualifying home-state compliance and reciprocity. This exam-preparation course is not being represented as approved renewal credit.

03 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

If a license expires

An expired license is not a grace period for selling. Maryland prohibits insurance business until reinstatement is effective. For up to one year after expiration, reinstatement requires the appropriate application, renewal fees, a one-hundred-dollar reinstatement fee and proof of required education. An application within sixty days leads to retroactive reinstatement under the statute. An application later than sixty days leads to prospective reinstatement. That distinction does not authorize someone to keep working while expired and simply hope to fix the record afterward.

04 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Keep contact and business details current

A licensee must report changes in legal name, trade name, email address or address within thirty days. Filing a trade name is not the same as obtaining new lines of authority. There is also a separate ten-day rule for changes to the designated business-entity contact's required information. Read the role named in the question before choosing the clock. Moving between states can create additional resident or nonresident requirements. Updating an agency website alone does not update the Administration's licensing record.

05 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Report the event the statute identifies

Two reporting rules are easy to confuse. A covered adverse administrative action taken by another jurisdiction or another Maryland government unit must be reported within thirty days after final disposition. A covered criminal prosecution must be reported within thirty days after the initial court appearance. The criminal rule does not generally wait for a conviction. Maryland excludes misdemeanor violations of its Vehicle Law from that particular criminal-reporting requirement. Use the actual statute and required documents for the event, instead of treating every legal matter as the same deadline.

06 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Build a reliable compliance calendar

Imagine Marcus changes his email, approaches renewal and receives notice of a covered proceeding. These create separate duties with separate starting dates. He updates the licensing record, completes approved education by the earlier deadline, submits renewal on time and reports the proceeding with the required records. A timely and complete renewal application can preserve the existing license until the Commissioner makes a final renewal decision. That protection is different from letting the license expire without applying. Identify the event, the person responsible and the deadline each time.

APPLY / IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Go one step deeper.

The current CE deadline controls this lesson

Insurance §10–116(a)(2) and MIA’s main producer renewal page specify fifteen days before expiration. An older MIA CE chart still states thirty days. This course uses the current statute, records the conflicting handout and does not silently mix the two rules. §10–408 is a public-adjuster provision, not the producer CE statute.

Example: For an expiration date thirty days away, the statutory producer CE deadline is fifteen days away, although earlier completion is sensible.

Special CE rules are not a blanket age exemption

The statute includes specified long-service provisions, limited-lines exceptions, special pre-need seller rules, waivers and nonresident reciprocity. In particular, a long-service provision requires the stated history and affidavit; simply reaching a certain birthday does not automatically remove CE.

Example: A new producer cannot claim an exemption just because an older colleague follows a different approved requirement.

Reinstatement details

The ordinary reinstatement window is up to one year after expiration. At sixty days or less the statute provides retroactive reinstatement; after sixty days it is prospective. After the one-year window, the person must use the initial-license pathway and meet applicable requirements. MIA currently lists $54 renewal, $15 fraud-prevention and $100 reinstatement fees for the ordinary reinstatement process; platform charges may be separate.

Example: Applying on day seventy does not make the reinstatement effective back on the original expiration date.

Moving across state lines

§10–119(g) provides a thirty-day certification/address duty for the specified producer who changes legal residence between states. A producer moving into Maryland must apply for resident authority within ninety days under §10–119(h); applicable same-line examination relief has conditions. These are different events, not interchangeable clocks.

Example: A nonresident who loses the home-state license cannot assume the Maryland nonresident license continues unaffected.

Supporting documents matter

§10–126 requires appropriate records with the reports: an adverse administrative action report includes the order, consent order or other relevant legal documents; the criminal report includes the charging document, any court order and other relevant legal documents.

Example: An internal message to a manager is not the required report to the Commissioner. The criminal-reporting exclusion covers misdemeanor violations of Maryland Vehicle Law or another jurisdiction’s vehicle law.
