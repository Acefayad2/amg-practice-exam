01 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

One master policy, individual protection

Elena's employer provides group life insurance. The employer or eligible trustees hold the master policy, while Elena is an insured employee. She receives a certificate describing her protection, who receives benefits and important rights. Under Maryland's employer-group rule, this coverage benefits people other than the employer. It is different from a business buying key-person coverage for its own loss. Eligible employees may include all employees or qualifying classes. Read the policy's eligibility terms rather than assuming that every worker, contractor or family member is automatically covered.

02 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Contributions and eligible dependents

Group premiums may come from the employer, employees or both. When employees contribute, the plan is contributory. When they do not, it is noncontributory. Maryland's employer-group rule generally requires coverage of all eligible employees in a noncontributory plan, except written rejections and the stated insurability exception. Covered arrangements may extend protection to a spouse, domestic partner or dependent children. The amount on a dependent cannot exceed the employee's amount under the rule taught here. A dependent's coverage and conversion rights require their own attention.

03 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Leaving employment can trigger conversion

Marcus leaves his job and loses the covered group life insurance. Maryland's conversion rule lets an eligible insured obtain individual life coverage from the insurer without new evidence of insurability. The ordinary application and first-premium deadline is thirty-one days after termination of employment or eligible membership. The available form is a form the insurer customarily issues, other than term insurance, subject to the stated conditions. Conversion preserves an opportunity for coverage, not the old group price. The premium reflects the permitted form, amount, risk class and attained age.

04 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Amount, notice and a death during conversion

Suppose Marcus loses eighty thousand dollars of group coverage but becomes eligible for thirty thousand dollars under another group policy within the stated thirty-one-day period. Under the Maryland conversion amount rule, the maximum is fifty thousand dollars. The statute also requires written notice of conversion rights and provides an extension when that notice is missing or late, within its outer limit. If an insured dies while entitled to convert but before the individual policy is effective, the amount available for conversion is payable under the group policy, even without the application or first premium.

05 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

The whole group policy ends

Now change the facts. The employer's entire group policy ends, or a covered class is removed, instead of Marcus simply leaving his job. A separate Maryland provision addresses this event. It protects an insured who has been covered for at least five years before termination, subject to the conversion conditions. The policy may limit the individual amount to the lesser of the lost coverage after the specified new-group offset, or ten thousand dollars. Do not apply this five-year requirement and possible cap to every ordinary employment-termination conversion question.

06 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Rights can be assigned

Maryland permits an insured to assign group-life rights and benefits under an arrangement among the insured, policyholder and insurer. The assigned rights can include conversion and naming a beneficiary. The assignee receives the rights actually assigned. Follow the arrangement and the insurer's notice process; do not assume that every assignment transfers every right. Elena should understand what she controls after an assignment and who can exercise the transferred rights. For an exam scenario, identify the event first, then the right, the responsible person, the amount and the deadline.

APPLY / IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Go one step deeper.

Dependent coverage has Maryland-specific definitions

For the covered group policies in §17–209, dependent children may include a child under eighteen and an older child attending an educational institution while relying on the employee or member for financial support. Domestic partner is defined by the policy. Spouse, domestic-partner and child coverage cannot exceed the employee’s or member’s amount.

Example: Do not assume a universal age-twenty-six cutoff applies to this group-life statute.

The conversion notice can extend the application period

Under §17–309, written notice of rights is due at least fifteen days before the ordinary conversion period expires. If that notice is not provided as required, the insured gets an additional fifteen days after receiving it to apply and pay, but the additional period cannot extend beyond sixty days after expiration of the ordinary conversion period. Read the actual notice facts before calculating a deadline.

Example: A missing notice does not create an unlimited right to wait before applying for the conversion.

Conversion does not carry every supplementary benefit

The statutory individual conversion is life coverage without disability or other supplementary benefits, on an available form other than term. Its premium is based on the form, amount, risk class and attained age as specified. The conversion amount is reduced by qualifying new group eligibility within thirty-one days.

Example: A departing employee should not assume the former group rate and every rider continue unchanged.

Grace period and incontestability have different purposes

The standard group provision gives the policyholder a thirty-one-day grace period after premiums other than the first, with the specified written-discontinuance exception. The group incontestability provision uses two years, with the nonpayment exception; an insured’s insurability statement is subject to the separate written, signed and lifetime-period conditions in §17–303.

Example: A grace period concerns timely premium payment; incontestability limits specified challenges to validity.

The standard-provision scope matters

§17–301 allows specified equally or more favorable provisions. It excludes creditor policies insuring debtors from §§17–307 through 17–311. Group life does not simply import every standard individual-life provision. Dependent conversion rights for covered arrangements are addressed in §17–209.

Example: An employer-group conversion example should not be automatically applied to a creditor’s group credit-life policy.

Assignment transfers specified rights

Under §17–104, the arrangement can assign any or all rights and benefits, including conversion and beneficiary designation. The insurer deals with the assignee according to that assignment and is protected as stated for a payment or conversion before receiving notice at its home office.

Example: An assignment of a particular benefit is not proof that every unrelated policy right changed hands.
