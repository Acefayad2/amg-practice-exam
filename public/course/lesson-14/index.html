01 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

A sales message must be accurate

Aisha sees an online post promising that a life policy is a risk-free savings account with guaranteed large returns. A polished design does not make that description accurate. Maryland prohibits untrue, deceptive or misleading insurance advertising and misleading policy descriptions. Identify the product as life insurance, explain material conditions and separate guarantees from assumptions. The same honesty belongs in a conversation, a social post and a presentation. An attractive result based on a possible future crediting rate should never be presented as a contractual promise.

02 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Read the two kinds of values

An illustration can project guaranteed and nonguaranteed policy values over time. The labels matter. Maryland requires the applicable guaranteed elements to appear before corresponding nonguaranteed elements. Nonguaranteed assumptions can change, and actual results can be higher or lower. Suppose a projected dividend is expected to help pay a future premium. That projection does not make the policy paid up. The rules prohibit misleading vanishing-premium language when it implies that nonguaranteed values eliminate the continuing obligation. Show what happens if the assumed results do not occur.

03 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Match the illustration to the policy

If a policy is applied for as illustrated, the signed basic illustration goes to the insurer with the application and a copy goes to the applicant. If the insurer issues different terms, a revised illustration must match the issued policy. It is labeled revised and signed and dated no later than delivery, with copies for the insurer and policyowner. Do not keep showing a more attractive old version. If no conforming illustration was used at application, follow the certification and delivery procedure required for that situation.

04 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Guide, summary and illustration

The buyer's guide gives general help with understanding and comparing life insurance. A policy summary supplies the specified costs and guaranteed benefits of the particular policy. An illustration has its own rules for depicting values over time. Maryland generally requires the buyer's guide before acceptance of the initial premium. If the policy has an unconditional refund provision of at least ten days, the guide may instead arrive with or before policy delivery. For covered forms not marketed with an illustration, the required policy summary follows the same delivery timing.

05 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Privacy involves purpose and permission

Elena collects income and other private information for a life application. That does not make the information available for any marketing purpose. Maryland's financial-privacy rules generally require a clear initial notice when the customer relationship starts. Before certain disclosures to a nonaffiliated third party, the licensee must provide the required notices and a reasonable opportunity to opt out. The customer must not have opted out. Exceptions exist for specified transaction servicing and other lawful purposes. Identify the information, recipient and purpose before deciding which rule applies.

06 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Apply exceptions carefully

Annual privacy notices are generally part of a continuing customer relationship, but Maryland has an exception when disclosures are limited to the specified permitted categories and the disclosed policies and practices have not changed. Nonpublic health information has a separate authorization rule with listed insurance-function exceptions, including underwriting and claims work. That matters even in a life-insurance application. An exception for a real underwriting task is not permission to post a client's diagnosis in a sales chat. Use approved systems, limit access and preserve the required records.

APPLY / IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Go one step deeper.

A complete illustration carries its assumptions

An illustration is labeled as life insurance and includes identifying policy, insurer, producer and proposed-insured information. Guaranteed values are clearly distinguished from nonguaranteed values. The latter cannot be described as certain, and an incomplete illustration is prohibited.

Example: Showing only the attractive nonguaranteed column conceals the information needed to understand the projection.

Revised terms require a revised presentation

COMAR 31.09.09.09 distinguishes a policy applied for as illustrated, one issued on different terms, and an application without a conforming illustration. The proper certification, signatures, copies and delivery process depend on that situation. The insurer retains the applicable signed illustrations and certifications until three years after the policy is no longer in force; no copy is required under that paragraph if no policy is issued.

Example: A higher issued premium requires a presentation that matches the issued contract, not the old estimate.

The summary explains the particular policy

For covered forms not marketed with an illustration, the summary shows guarantees only. Required information includes the carrier and producer details, policy and rider types, specified premium and benefit patterns, guaranteed cash surrender values and applicable loan-interest information. Costs are shown as totals rather than only per-unit figures.

Example: A customer comparing two policies needs the actual annual costs and benefits, not simply the smallest quoted cost per thousand dollars.

Initial and annual notices are different

An initial notice generally accompanies establishment of the continuing customer relationship, subject to the regulation’s specific exceptions. Annual notice has a separate exception when only the specified permitted disclosure categories are used and the previously disclosed policies and practices have not changed. A former customer without a continuing relationship does not require the annual notice under this rule.

Example: Do not answer that a notice is always required every year regardless of the exception facts.

An opt-out opportunity must be meaningful

Before disclosures governed by COMAR 31.16.08.11, provide the required initial and opt-out notices, allow a reasonable opportunity and respect the consumer’s decision. The rule gives examples, including a thirty-day response period for mailed notices. Specific servicing, legal and other exceptions have their own conditions.

Example: Selling a client list to an unrelated marketer is a different purpose from sending information needed to service the client’s requested policy.

Life underwriting can involve health information

This remains a life-only course. Health information appears because a life application can involve medical facts. COMAR 31.16.08.17 supplies an authorization rule and enumerated insurance-function exceptions. Other applicable laws and consent rules must also be considered; this single exception list does not settle every privacy question.

Example: Use the authorized underwriting channel to transmit requested records instead of sharing them with unrelated sales staff.
