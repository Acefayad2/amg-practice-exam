01 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Ownership identifies who can exercise rights

Elena owns a policy on Marcus's life and names their adult daughter as revocable beneficiary. Elena controls the policyowner's rights under the contract. Marcus is the insured, and the daughter is the designated recipient of a covered death benefit. The person sending premium payments does not automatically become the owner. Owner rights can include changing a revocable beneficiary, assigning the contract, choosing available options and accessing policy values when the contract provides them. Each right remains subject to policy terms and other valid interests. A term policy does not acquire a cash value merely because its owner asks for a loan.

02 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

A beneficiary’s status can limit an owner’s action

With a revocable designation, the reserved right to change beneficiaries ordinarily remains with the owner. An irrevocable designation creates an interest that can restrict changes without the required consent. An existing assignment can also affect what the owner may do. Maryland permits the policy to require insurer endorsement or acceptance before a beneficiary change binds the insurer. A verbal request to a producer is not a reliable substitute for completing the required process. Identify the current owner, designation and assignments first, then obtain the proper documents before describing the change as complete.

03 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Absolute assignment transfers ownership rights

Suppose Elena executes a valid absolute assignment to her adult daughter. An absolute assignment transfers the ownership rights described by that complete transfer. The daughter becomes the new owner through the required process. This is different from naming her beneficiary, which only addresses who receives an applicable benefit. Changing ownership does not ordinarily change the person whose life is insured. It also does not erase existing policy limitations, loans or superior interests. The parties should review the actual assignment and obtain any required consent or recording. A transfer can have tax consequences that deserve separate review.

04 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Collateral assignment secures a debt

Now assume Elena retains ownership but pledges specified policy rights to a lender as collateral. A collateral assignment secures an obligation instead of simply giving the lender the whole beneficial interest forever. Read the document: it can grant enforcement rights and limit the owner's actions while the debt exists. In a simplified covered death claim, the payable benefit is one hundred thousand dollars and the secured amount due is twenty thousand dollars. Assume no other claims or deductions. The lender receives twenty thousand dollars and the designated beneficiary receives the remaining eighty thousand dollars.

05 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Release the security when the obligation ends

After the secured obligation is satisfied, obtain the appropriate release of the collateral assignment and follow the insurer's recording process. Do not assume the company's records update because the lender cashed a final payment. An assignment can be subject to an insurer's prior lien and specific reserved rights. Its exact wording matters more than a blanket statement that every assignment transfers every right. An insurer's acknowledgment generally records the transaction under its process; it is not a promise that every legal or tax consequence has been reviewed. Keep copies and confirm who can exercise each affected right.

06 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

The entire contract identifies the governing documents

Maryland's individual life provision treats the policy, or the policy with an endorsed or attached application, as the entire contract between the parties. Where the application is included, its statements are representations rather than warranties in the absence of fraud. Required riders and endorsements form part of the actual contract as incorporated. Sales material is separate from the policy. A producer cannot amend the coverage through an informal promise. Check the documents, insured amount, options and accepted changes. Application copies and corrections also have Maryland rules. Use the actual contract to resolve rights instead of relying on a remembered sales conversation.

APPLY / IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Go one step deeper.

Control follows the right, not just a name

A single person may be owner, insured and payer, but the roles can be held by different people. Rights are limited by the actual design, irrevocable interests and valid assignments. A cash-value withdrawal right is irrelevant to a term policy without cash value.

Example: A relative who makes a premium payment does not thereby become the owner.

Use the actual beneficiary-change process

Maryland §16-212 reserves the right to designate or change a beneficiary unless the designation is irrevocable. The policy can require endorsement or insurer acceptance before a change binds the insurer. Required consents and valid assignments must also be considered.

Example: A draft beneficiary form left unsigned in a drawer is not a completed change through the stated process.

Absolute and collateral assignments have different aims

An absolute assignment transfers ownership rights. A collateral assignment grants rights as security for an obligation and can include substantial enforcement rights while reserving others. It does not necessarily change the insured or the named beneficiary. Read the form rather than promising the owner retains unrestricted control.

Example: The carrier’s sample collateral form reserves a beneficiary-change right but grants specified proceeds, surrender and borrowing rights to the assignee, subject to its terms.

Compute the claim from the stated payable amount

The lesson assumes a $100,000 payable benefit, a $20,000 secured amount due, no prior liens and no other deductions. It pays the lender $20,000 and the beneficiary $80,000. A real claim requires checking the secured balance, interest, prior policy loans and assignment priority.

Example: If the secured amount due were $12,000 under those same simplified assumptions, the remaining beneficiary payment would be $88,000.

Keep application evidence and corrections separate

Under §12-206, an issuance application generally must be attached or otherwise made part of the issued contract to be admissible in a related proceeding, with the stated industrial-life exception. A requested renewal or reinstatement application copy has a separate 30-day delivery rule and stated beneficiary-interest condition. Someone other than the applicant generally cannot alter the written application without written consent; an insurer may make a clearly identified administrative insertion.

Example: An administrative insertion must not falsely appear to be the applicant’s own answer.

Standard individual provisions have a defined scope

Section 16-201 excludes group life, pure endowments and annuity contracts from this individual-life standard-provision requirement. It also specifies treatment of disability/accidental-death supplementary provisions and inapplicable single-premium or term provisions. Group contracts have separate standard provisions.

Example: Do not automatically apply the individual-life entire-contract statute to every annuity or group question merely because the issuer is a life insurer.
