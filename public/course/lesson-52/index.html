01 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Annuitization changes the tax calculation

Rosa converts a personally owned nonqualified annuity to fixed lifetime payments. This is annuity income, not an ordinary partial withdrawal before payments start. Under the applicable General Rule, part of each payment can recover her investment and the balance can be taxable. Suppose the question gives one hundred thousand dollars of adjusted investment and two hundred thousand dollars of expected return for the calculation. Dividing investment by expected return gives a fifty percent exclusion percentage. Expected return is a tax calculation using the applicable payment terms and factors; it is not simply a promised investment profit. Use the supplied facts rather than guessing a life expectancy.

02 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Investment cannot be recovered tax-free twice

Assume the fixed annual payment is twenty thousand dollars and the stated exclusion percentage is fifty percent. Ten thousand dollars recovers investment and ten thousand dollars is taxable each full year while unrecovered investment remains under these simplified facts. If the annuity starting date is in twenty twenty-six, total excluded payments cannot exceed the one hundred thousand dollar investment. Once that amount has been recovered, later payments are fully taxable under the stated rule. Lifetime payments can continue after investment is recovered; the tax treatment changes, not the lifetime promise. Variable payments use a different General Rule computation, so do not apply this fixed-payment ratio to every design.

03 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

An annuity death payment can contain taxable earnings

David dies before his individually owned nonqualified annuity begins income payments. Assume a beneficiary receives one hundred twenty thousand dollars in one sum and the contract has eighty thousand dollars of unrecovered investment, with no other adjustment. Forty thousand dollars is the earnings portion and generally is taxable ordinary income. The beneficiary does not exclude the entire payment merely because it follows a death. Compare this with qualifying life insurance death proceeds, which follow a different exclusion rule. The contract's death-benefit formula establishes the amount payable. Tax law then determines the treatment of that amount. Those are two separate calculations, even when one insurer issues both types of products.

04 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

The distribution deadline depends on when the owner dies

For the modern nonqualified contract in this example, an owner's death before the annuity starting date generally requires distribution of the entire interest within five years. A qualifying individual beneficiary can use the statutory life-expectancy alternative when its requirements are met, including timely commencement generally within one year. A surviving spouse designated as beneficiary can have a continuation option under the law. If death occurs after the annuity starting date, remaining payments generally must be made at least as rapidly as the existing method. These rules do not guarantee every payout option in every contract. They also are not the separate inherited-account rules for individual retirement arrangements.

05 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Naming a human annuitant does not make a corporation human

Consider a corporation that buys an annuity with company money and owns all the contract rights. Naming David as the annuitant does not make him the owner. Under the general non-natural-owner rule, income on a nonqualified annuity held by a corporation or other non-natural person can be taxable currently as ordinary income, rather than receiving the usual individual-owner deferral. The law has specific exceptions. These include certain qualified arrangements, a qualifying immediate annuity and contracts acquired by an estate because of a death. A trust or entity genuinely holding as an agent for a natural person requires its actual ownership facts to be examined. Do not classify every trust the same way.

06 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Match the rule to the event before selecting an answer

Here are four questions that organize the analysis. Is the payment a withdrawal, annuity income or a death distribution? How much investment remains to be recovered? Who is the owner, and what tax arrangement holds the contract? Which timing requirement or exception applies to these facts? Rosa's fixed income example uses a stated exclusion calculation. David's beneficiary example separates earnings from unrecovered investment. The corporation example begins with ownership and the statutory exceptions. None can be solved by memorizing that all annuities are tax-free, or that every payment is fully taxable. The correct answer comes from matching the event and ownership facts with the applicable rule.

APPLY / IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Go one step deeper.

Fixed income example and variable payments differ

The lesson assumes a fixed-payment nonqualified annuity beginning in 2026, a supplied $100,000 adjusted investment and $200,000 expected return. Expected return is not a rate of investment growth. The General Rule for variable payments instead determines a tax-free dollar amount using investment and the expected number of payments, subject to its applicable adjustments.

Example: Qualified-plan annuities can use the Simplified Method; do not automatically use this personal-annuity example.

Read §72(s) within its scope

For these nonqualified contracts, death before the annuity starting date ordinarily triggers the five-year distribution rule. The designated-individual alternative requires distributions over life or a period no longer than life expectancy, with commencement generally within one year, subject to regulatory rules. A spouse beneficiary can be treated as owner. After the starting date, remaining distributions must be at least as rapid as the existing method. Qualified plans and IRAs have their own applicable rules.

Example: For a nonindividual owner within this rule, the primary annuitant is treated as the owner; changing the primary annuitant can be treated as death.

The corporate-owner restriction has actual exceptions

Section 72(u) generally taxes annual contract income for a non-natural owner. An entity holding as agent for a natural person is treated differently under that rule. Listed exceptions include qualifying retirement arrangements, an estate’s acquisition by reason of death, specified employer-held or qualified-funding arrangements, and an immediate annuity meeting the statutory definition.

Example: For this exception, an immediate annuity uses a single premium, starts no later than one year after purchase, and provides substantially equal payments at least annually.

Benefit entitlement is separate from tax treatment

An annuity’s accumulation-stage death benefit can include both investment and earnings. Beneficiary continuation, contract payout options and the tax distribution deadline each need review. A tax rule does not create a contractual benefit that was not purchased.

Example: The $120,000 single-sum example assumes $80,000 unrecovered investment and no other adjustment.
