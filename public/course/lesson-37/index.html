01 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

A cash-value policy can offer more than cancellation

Marcus can no longer afford the continuing premiums on his whole life policy. Before he gives it up, he reviews its nonforfeiture choices. These choices use the value already built under the policy when premium payments stop. The familiar alternatives are taking available cash, using value for extended term insurance, or keeping a smaller amount of paid-up permanent insurance. They preserve different things. A no-cash-value term policy does not suddenly acquire those values because its premium is missed. Start with the actual policy, current values and any outstanding debt. Then compare what protection would remain under each available choice.

02 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Cash surrender ends the surrendered protection

Cash surrender pays the available surrender amount to the owner and ends the surrendered policy's protection. It is not an advance death claim. Suppose the insurer's statement shows twelve thousand dollars available before a separately listed two thousand dollar policy debt, with no other adjustment. The net cash payment is ten thousand dollars. If the quoted amount already includes that debt, do not deduct it again. Surrender can also have tax consequences, addressed in the tax lessons. The owner should understand the lost death protection before signing. A later change of mind does not create an automatic right to reinstate a cash-surrendered policy.

03 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Extended term trades permanent duration for time

Under the simplified extended-term choice, the available value buys term protection at the original face amount for as long as that value purchases. No further premiums are required for that purchased period. Assume a debt-free policy with a one hundred thousand dollar face amount. Its actual table offers one hundred thousand dollars of extended term for eight years. A covered death in year seven falls within that period. Death in year nine does not. Eight years comes from this hypothetical table, not a universal formula. Debt, additions and the contract can affect the actual benefit and duration.

04 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Reduced paid-up trades face amount for permanence

Reduced paid-up insurance uses available value to continue a smaller amount of permanent life protection with no further required premiums for that reduced base benefit. Assume the same policy offers thirty-two thousand dollars of reduced paid-up whole life. The family gives up part of the former one hundred thousand dollar amount but keeps the smaller permanent benefit. This is different from paying the final scheduled premium on a limited-pay contract and keeping its original promised amount. It is also different from purchasing paid-up additions with a dividend. Review whether riders continue and how debt affects the actual option.

05 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Read the election deadline and automatic default

Maryland's standard provision for these choices gives the specified election opportunity within sixty days after the due date of the premium in default. If the entitled person does not choose another available option in that period, the policy's specified paid-up benefit takes effect. Do not automatically name extended term as the required default for every Maryland policy. Read the policy's own provision and the statute's applicability rules. The standard statute also distinguishes paid-up benefit eligibility from ordinary and industrial cash-surrender minimums. A more favorable contract may provide value earlier. Contact the insurer promptly while the choice remains available.

06 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Match the choice to the stated objective

Marcus compares the three hypothetical offers. If his stated priority is ten thousand dollars of immediate net cash and ending this protection, cash surrender matches that objective. If he wants the stated one hundred thousand dollar benefit for the next eight years without more premiums, extended term matches it. If he wants some permanent protection without continuing premiums, the thirty-two thousand dollar reduced paid-up option matches that goal. Those are exam comparisons using supplied facts, not a recommendation for every family. Review current protection needs, remaining resources, tax effects and the actual written values before an owner makes a real decision.

APPLY / IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Go one step deeper.

Nonforfeiture rights depend on the applicable insurance plan

Maryland’s life nonforfeiture subtitle has specific exclusions under §16-301, including group life, annuities and certain term designs. Section 16-304 allows omission of a required provision to the extent it does not apply to the plan. Ordinary term without cash value does not supply a cash-value nonforfeiture fund.

Example: First identify the policy design and the actual guaranteed values.

Do not treat cash value as the entire death benefit

A $100,000 face amount does not mean $100,000 can be taken on surrender. Use the insurer’s current surrender quotation, its treatment of policy debt, additions and any applicable charges. Do not subtract an item twice if the quote is already net of that item.

Example: A quote of $9,500 already net of a $1,500 loan still pays $9,500 under the stated facts.

Use the actual table for extended-term duration

The common debt-free example keeps the original face amount temporarily, using available value to buy the stated term period. Age, available value, debt, additions and the applicable actuarial terms affect real values. The course’s eight years is an explicit hypothetical offer.

Example: More years cannot be inferred merely because the original policy was called whole life.

Reduced paid-up is different from a paid-up addition

Reduced paid-up nonforfeiture continues a smaller permanent base benefit after the owner stops premiums. A paid-up addition is an extra amount purchased under the policy, often with dividends. Completion of a limited-pay schedule is another distinct way a policy can become paid up.

Example: A reduction from $100,000 to $32,000 is not a new $32,000 addition on top of the original amount.

Maryland’s statutory minimums are not promises of zero earlier value

Section 16-303(b) addresses a paid-up benefit after at least one year of premiums, with the specified request within sixty days of default. Its cash-surrender alternative after default distinguishes at least three years of premiums for ordinary life and five for industrial life. The statute permits corresponding provisions at least as favorable, and the contract may offer value earlier.

Example: Read the actual table rather than announcing that every policy has no surrender value until its third anniversary.

Default and payment timing have separate rules

Section 16-303(c) calls for the specified paid-up nonforfeiture benefit when another available option is not elected within sixty days after the defaulted premium’s due date. Section 16-304(b) reserves up to six months to defer cash-surrender payment after the stated demand and surrender. A cash surrender also defeats the standard §16-210 reinstatement provision.

Example: An election window, a payment deferral and a reinstatement condition are different questions.
