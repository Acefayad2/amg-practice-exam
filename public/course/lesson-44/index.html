01 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

The concern changes from early death to long life

Rosa and David are approaching retirement. They already understand life insurance as protection against the financial consequences of a covered death. Now they ask how to create income if they live much longer than expected. An annuity is a contract with an insurance company that can provide payments immediately or at a later time. A lifetime income option addresses longevity risk: the risk of outliving income resources. Not every annuity payment option lasts for life. A fixed period of payments can end while the recipient is still living. Identify the promised income pattern before assuming that every annuity eliminates that risk.

Financial protection after covered death

Lifetime annuity income

Payments while the covered life continues

Fixed-period income

Ends after the stated period

02 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Accumulation is the stage before income conversion

During a deferred annuity's accumulation phase, the contract holds value under its crediting or investment provisions. Rosa may fund a contract with one premium or a permitted series of premiums. The value can change with interest, investment performance, charges and withdrawals, depending on the design. Accumulation does not mean all annuities earn the same guaranteed rate. It also does not mean money is freely accessible without consequences. A withdrawal can involve surrender charges, adjustments and tax effects. Track the contract value and the amount available on surrender separately. The accumulation stage describes where the contract is in its operation, not which investment guarantees it contains.

One premium or permitted payments

Accumulation

03 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Annuitization creates the chosen payment stream

Annuitization converts the applicable value into a series of payments under the selected settlement option. The option determines whether income lasts for one life, two lives or a stated period, and whether anything continues after a death. This decision often limits access to the amount converted and can be difficult or impossible to reverse under the contract. A monthly withdrawal from an account is not automatically annuitization. Some optional lifetime-withdrawal benefits operate differently from converting the contract into annuity payments. For this lesson, separate accumulating value, taking a withdrawal and formally choosing an annuity income option. The next lessons compare the payment patterns in detail.

Maintain value under contract terms

Withdrawal

Take money from available contract value

Annuitization

Convert value into a selected income stream

04 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

The owner controls rights; the annuitant supplies the life

The owner holds the contractual decision rights, subject to the contract and applicable law. These can include selecting beneficiaries, requesting allowed withdrawals and choosing the income option. The annuitant is the person whose life is used for the contract's life-based calculations or payments. The owner and annuitant may be the same person, but the roles are not identical. Suppose a permitted contract names Rosa as owner and David as annuitant. Rosa exercises the owner's permitted rights; David's life is the measuring life for the stated life-contingent benefit. Being the annuitant alone does not automatically give David every ownership right. Always read who holds each role.

05 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

A beneficiary receives what the contract actually provides

Rosa names their adult daughter, Maya, as beneficiary. That designation identifies who receives an applicable death benefit or remaining guaranteed payments under the contract. It does not automatically promise repayment of every premium in every phase. During accumulation, the contract's death provisions matter. After annuitization, the selected income option matters: a life-only option may end at death, while a period-certain or refund feature can protect specified payments for a beneficiary. Also check whose death triggers the provision, because owner and annuitant rules can differ. A beneficiary designation identifies the recipient of an available benefit; it does not create a benefit that the selected contract does not provide.

06 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Start with the household’s needs and available money

Before recommending an annuity, identify Rosa and David's income needs, other resources, time horizon, risk tolerance and need for accessible cash. A long surrender period can conflict with money needed soon. A lifetime promise can be useful, but it must be evaluated together with costs, limits, inflation exposure and the insurer's ability to meet its obligations. A product guarantee does not make every contract appropriate for every retiree. Maryland's recommendation requirements receive a dedicated lesson later in this course. Here, connect the basic structure to the client: who controls it, whose life matters, who may receive a death benefit, and when the money becomes income.

APPLY / IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Go one step deeper.

The insurance function is protection against longevity risk

A life-contingent annuity pools longevity experience to support payments for the covered lifetime. An option paying for exactly ten years provides a specified duration rather than a promise for every remaining year of life.

Example: Living to age 100 does not extend a fixed ten-year-only payment promise.

Accumulation and product guarantees are different classifications

A deferred annuity’s accumulation value follows its actual fixed, indexed or variable design. Fees, withdrawals and adjustments can affect available value. Single-premium and flexible-premium funding are separate classifications from immediate versus deferred income.

Example: A single deposit can fund a deferred contract.

Distinguish annuitization from systematic withdrawals

Formal annuitization establishes payments under the chosen option. Ordinary withdrawals and optional guaranteed lifetime withdrawal riders have different operations and limits. Do not assume that every monthly check means the owner has annuitized.

Example: Check the election and the contract’s continued access rules, rather than the payment frequency alone.

A person can hold more than one role

The owner exercises permitted contract rights. The annuitant supplies the measuring life for life-based benefits. The beneficiary receives applicable benefits under the death or continuation provisions. The Florida source is cited for general terminology only; its state-specific deadlines and tax summaries are not Maryland rules.

Example: Rosa can be both owner and annuitant while Maya is beneficiary, or an eligible contract may use different people.

Death benefits depend on phase and trigger

Read whether the relevant provision is triggered by an owner’s death, an annuitant’s death or another stated event. After income begins, a life-only, guaranteed-period, survivor or refund election produces different outcomes.

Example: Naming Maya does not turn a life-only income option into a refund option.

Suitability starts with the actual consumer profile

A recommendation must account for the consumer’s circumstances and the product’s costs, benefits and restrictions. Maryland’s current best-interest duties are taught in Part 48. Product guarantees depend on the insurer’s financial strength and claims-paying ability.

Example: Money reserved for a near-term essential expense requires a liquidity review before a long-term commitment.
