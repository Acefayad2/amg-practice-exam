01 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

A policy loan uses the policy as security

Marcus wants money for a family expense while keeping his life policy. If his contract has sufficient available loan value and he has the required rights, he can request a policy loan. The insurer advances money with the policy as security. This is different from an unrelated bank loan and different from withdrawing part of the policy's value. Policy debt carries interest and affects the amount ultimately available on surrender or death. The face amount alone does not establish how much Marcus can borrow. Read the current loan value, existing debt, applicable rate and conditions before explaining the available amount.

Security

The life policy supports the advance

Limit

Available loan value, not the face amount

Cost

Interest and effects of outstanding debt

02 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Count principal and unpaid interest at the claim

Assume a covered death benefit of two hundred thousand dollars. The policy has twenty thousand dollars of unpaid loan principal and one thousand dollars of accrued unpaid loan interest. With no other adjustments, the net benefit is one hundred seventy-nine thousand dollars. Leaving out the interest would overstate the payment. A policy loan does not always require scheduled repayment like a bank installment loan, but unpaid debt still has consequences. Interest can be added to principal under the contract. If debt consumes the available value, the policy can face termination under the applicable conditions and notice requirements. Monitor the actual statements.

03 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

An automatic premium loan pays a premium with debt

Marcus misses a premium after previously electing his policy's automatic premium loan provision. If the provision's conditions are met and enough value is available, the insurer makes a loan to pay that premium. The protection can continue, but the owner now owes policy debt and interest. This is not a free premium waiver. Maryland requires policies subject to its loan section to provide this provision, except the stated weekly-premium category, and makes its use subject to the entitled person's election. The section excludes term policies, term riders and industrial life. Do not present it as an automatic benefit on every life policy.

04 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

A withdrawal removes value instead of creating a loan

Elena requests a partial withdrawal from a universal life policy that permits it. A withdrawal takes value out permanently under that transaction instead of recording a loan balance to repay. It may reduce the death benefit, incur charges and make future funding less secure under the contract. A full surrender instead ends the surrendered policy's protection. Whole life may allow access through different features, such as surrendering paid-up additions, rather than the same universal-life withdrawal mechanism. Identify the actual feature before quoting an effect. The same five thousand dollar cash need can produce different continuing obligations under a loan and a withdrawal.

Loan

Debt plus interest; repayment can reduce debt

Partial withdrawal

Value removed under the permitted feature

Full surrender

Surrendered protection ends

05 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Using a loan for education does not change its mechanics

Marcus considers borrowing five thousand dollars against available policy value to help pay tuition. Paying a college bill does not transform that advance into a scholarship, federal student loan or tax-favored education account. It remains a policy loan with its own interest, effect on benefits and risk if debt grows. Compare the family's remaining protection needs and other funding resources before committing. The word educational describes the intended use in this example, not a universal separate policy rider. A producer should explain the real terms and avoid promising unlimited or cost-free access to the policy's death benefit.

06 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Read Maryland’s interest and notice rules carefully

Maryland's modern policy-loan interest provision permits either the specified fixed maximum or an adjustable maximum calculated under the statute. Do not call six percent the universal cap for every policy loan. The contract must identify its applicable rate mechanism. Adjustable-rate loans have notice and adjustment rules. The standard loan provision also addresses termination when total debt reaches the loan value, including at least thirty days' notice to the specified parties. A loan for paying a premium is treated differently from other loans under the six-month deferral provision. Identify the transaction and controlling section before choosing a deadline or interest rule.

APPLY / IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Go one step deeper.

Maryland’s statutory loan conditions have a defined scope

Section 16-207 excludes term policies, term-rider benefits and industrial life. For covered policies it addresses advances on proper assignment or pledge, using the policy as sole security, after at least three years of premiums, after cash surrender value exists and while no premium remains in default beyond grace. A contract may provide more favorable access earlier. Section 16-201 supplies the wider standard-provision scope.

Example: An available-value loan feature from issue in a particular policy is not proof that Maryland requires every policy to lend immediately.

Do not subtract the same debt twice

Section 16-207(d) describes the loan-value minimum and permitted deductions for existing debt, current-year unpaid premium and interest to the policy-year end. Read whether the supplied figure is gross or already net. Net death or surrender proceeds also reflect the applicable unpaid principal and interest.

Example: A $250,000 covered benefit less $15,000 principal and $900 unpaid interest gives $234,100 when no other adjustment applies.

Provision required is different from use required

Under §16-207(g), policies subject to the section must provide an automatic premium loan except the stated weekly-premium category. Use is subject to an election by the person entitled to elect. The loan also requires sufficient available value and the contract’s trigger. The outline’s automatic-option wording is taught through this explicit Maryland statute rather than as a universal forced loan.

Example: A required provision does not mean the insurer can disregard the owner’s election or lend beyond available value.

An automatic premium loan is not nonforfeiture or disability waiver

An automatic premium loan creates debt to fund the qualifying premium. Extended term or reduced paid-up nonforfeiture instead uses available value for a different paid-up benefit. A disability waiver operates under a rider’s qualifying conditions without making the waived amount an automatic premium loan.

Example: For an elected $600 premium loan and a hypothetical 5% simple full-year charge, the initial $600 debt plus $30 interest totals $630 if nothing is repaid.

Policy-loan rate limits must be read together

For policies issued on or after July 1, 1983, §16-208(b) permits a fixed maximum effective rate no greater than 8%, or an adjustable maximum no greater than the larger of the defined published monthly average and the cash-value calculation rate plus one percentage point. The adjustable maximum is determined at least annually but no more often than every three months. A qualifying 0.5-percentage-point increase is permitted; a qualifying decrease is required.

Example: The older §16-207 rate text is expressly subject to §16-208. Do not teach a universal 6% ceiling.

Notice, termination and deferral are separate matters

Section 16-207(e) permits the stated termination when total debt, including due or accrued interest, reaches the loan value and the insurer mails at least thirty days’ notice to the last known address of the insured or owner and the recorded assignee. Subsection (f) allows six months to defer a loan other than one used to pay the insurer a premium. Section 16-208 supplies adjustable-rate notices and protection against termination solely from an interest-rate change during the policy year.

Example: The cash-loan deferral is not a six-month waiting period before every automatic premium loan.

A partial withdrawal can affect future funding

Under a permitted universal-life withdrawal, value is removed and charges may apply. The actual death-benefit option and terms determine the benefit reduction. Whole-life access may use paid-up additions or another available feature. Reduced remaining value can increase lapse risk. Tax consequences are covered separately, including modified endowment contracts.

Example: Do not promise that an ordinary withdrawal can be reversed merely by labeling a later payment loan repayment.

An educational use is still an insurance transaction

A cash-value advance used for tuition keeps its policy-loan interest, security and benefit consequences. This lesson does not claim a distinct mandatory education-loan rider, student-aid eligibility or special tax treatment based only on the purpose.

Example: The family must consider both the bill being funded and the protection available after borrowing.
