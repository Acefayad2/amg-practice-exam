THE AGENT’S STARTING POINT

The concern is the financial effect of Marcus’s death on the people who rely on him. Life insurance can provide a contractual death benefit to beneficiaries. It does not prevent death or promise to cover every expense.

02 / FIVE USEFUL WORDS

Same situation. Different meanings.

Select each term to connect it to Marcus’s situation. On an exam, identify what the question is describing before you look at the answer choices.

A hazard changes the chance.

A dangerous working condition can make a fatal accident more likely.

→

A peril causes the loss.

For the life-insurance concern here, Marcus’s death is the peril. Losing his financial support is a consequence.

Death is certain eventually; its timing and financial effects are uncertain. That is why life insurance can address a risk even though everyone eventually dies.

03 / TELL HAZARDS APART

Condition, dishonesty or carelessness?

These three labels describe different reasons the chance of loss may increase. The examples below are separate fictional situations.

01

Physical hazard

A tangible condition that increases the likelihood or severity of loss.

Example: defective safety equipment at a worksite.

02

Moral hazard

Dishonesty or an intention to deceive associated with insurance loss.

Example: deliberately falsifying a death certificate to obtain a life-insurance payment.

03

Morale hazard

Carelessness or indifference because someone feels protected by insurance.

Example: ignoring safety precautions because “my insurance will take care of it.”

WATCH THE EXTRA “E”

Moral: deliberate dishonesty. Morale: a careless attitude. Read the behavior in the question; do not decide from the person’s occupation or background.

One more distinction: pure and speculative risk

PURE RISK

Loss or no loss

There is no opportunity for economic gain from the risk itself. A family’s exposure to losing an income earner’s support is an example.

SPECULATIVE RISK

A chance of gain or loss

Buying a stock hoping its price will rise is an example. A life policy does not insure against an investment falling in value.

“No gain” describes the underlying risk, not the absence of an insurance benefit. Not every pure risk is insurable. We will cover the requirements for insurable risks in a later lesson.

05 / RECAP & REVIEW

Keep these connections.

Risk is uncertainty about loss. Exposure is being subject to possible loss. A hazard increases the chance or severity. A peril causes the loss. The loss is the harm that results.

Buying life insurance is a way to transfer specified financial risk to an insurer under a contract. It changes who bears covered financial consequences; it does not remove the underlying possibility of death.
