01 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

The same policy can produce different tax events

Marcus hears that life insurance is tax-free and assumes every transaction receives the same treatment. Start by identifying the event. Growth inside a qualifying life policy generally is not taxed to the owner each year merely because cash value increased. Personal life insurance premiums generally are paid with after-tax money and are not deductible. Taking a distribution, surrendering the policy, receiving a death benefit and calculating an estate are different events with different rules. This lesson uses personally owned coverage meeting the federal life insurance definition. Modified endowment contracts receive a separate lesson because their distribution rules differ. Never replace those distinctions with a promise that all money is tax-free.

02 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

A premium refund is different from interest earned

A participating policy dividend generally represents a return of premium for federal income-tax purposes, to the extent it does not exceed the owner's remaining investment in the contract. That investment is often called basis. Prior tax-free returns can reduce basis, so use the adjusted figure rather than blindly adding every historical premium. If Marcus leaves dividends with the insurer to earn interest, the added interest generally is taxable even when the underlying dividend is a nontaxable premium return. Assume a stated eight hundred dollar dividend is entirely within unrecovered premiums and separately earns forty dollars of interest. The forty dollars is interest income under these assumptions.

03 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Borrowing and withdrawing do not work the same way

For an in-force nonmodified life contract, an ordinary policy loan generally is indebtedness rather than current taxable income. Interest and policy debt still matter. A withdrawal generally recovers basis before gain under the ordinary rule, but special rules can apply, including certain early benefit reductions. Do not assume every withdrawal receives identical treatment. If the policy later lapses or is surrendered with a loan outstanding, discharge of that debt can count toward the taxable distribution. Income can arise even when little or no new cash reaches Marcus. Keeping the policy in force and monitoring debt is therefore different from simply calling the original loan tax-free forever.

04 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

On surrender, compare proceeds with adjusted basis

Assume Marcus fully surrenders a policy and receives fifty-five thousand dollars. His adjusted investment in the contract is forty thousand dollars, and the example has no outstanding loan, other payment or further adjustment. The fifteen thousand dollar excess is ordinary income. The forty thousand dollars of recovered basis is not taxed again. This is not a death benefit, and keeping the policy for many years does not turn its surrender gain into a capital gain. In a different case with policy debt, the amount treated as received can include money used to discharge the loan, so the cash check alone may not show the entire taxable event.

05 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

A death benefit and its interest are separate amounts

Elena receives a qualifying two hundred thousand dollar death benefit plus three thousand dollars of separately stated interest. Assuming no exception to the ordinary death-benefit exclusion, the benefit is excluded from federal gross income and the interest is taxable. Choosing installments does not make all later interest disappear. Suppose the insurer holds a two hundred thousand dollar benefit for one hundred installments of twenty-four hundred dollars each. Under the stated fixed-installment calculation, two thousand dollars of each payment recovers the benefit, and four hundred dollars is interest. Life-contingent installments use additional calculations. Also check transfer-for-value and employer-owned coverage rules before assuming every death payment qualifies for the full exclusion.

06 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Outside probate does not mean outside the taxable estate

Now ask a different question: whether insurance is included in Marcus's federal gross estate. Proceeds payable to his estate are generally included. Proceeds payable to another beneficiary can also be included if he retained incidents of ownership at death, such as the power to change beneficiaries, surrender or borrow against the policy. Certain transfers within three years of death can also bring proceeds into the estate. Direct payment to Elena can avoid ordinary probate administration while still requiring an estate-tax analysis. Inclusion does not automatically mean tax is owed, because deductions and applicable exclusions also matter. Keep the recipient, income-tax result, probate treatment and estate inclusion as four separate questions.

APPLY / IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Go one step deeper.

Scope of these examples

The examples assume a contract qualifying as life insurance for federal tax purposes. A personal premium generally is not deductible, and inside buildup generally is tax-deferred. The amount and timing of an actual distribution matter. Business-owned coverage, modified endowment contracts and other exceptions require their own rules.

Example: An increase on an annual cash-value statement is not the same event as a full cash surrender.

Basis must be adjusted

Investment in the contract generally starts with premiums or other consideration, adjusted for applicable prior tax-free amounts and other required items. A nontaxable dividend received as a premium return can reduce the remaining investment. Dividends retained and applied as premiums have distinct rules. Use the stated adjusted basis in an exam calculation.

Example: Do not subtract a policy loan twice when the facts already provide an adjusted distribution or basis figure.

Ordinary dividends and deposited-dividend interest

A dividend is generally a premium return until the relevant investment is recovered. Interest credited on deposited ordinary insurance dividends is generally taxable separately. Veterans’ insurance interest has special federal treatment; do not generalize that exception to commercial life policies.

Example: The example’s $800 eligible premium return and $40 interest have different tax classifications.

Loans, withdrawals and lapse

Under ordinary non-MEC rules, an in-force policy loan is generally debt. A withdrawal ordinarily recovers investment first, but benefit-reduction rules during the first 15 years can alter that result. Lapse or surrender can treat an outstanding loan and interest discharge as a distribution, creating taxable gain without equivalent new cash.

Example: A loan described as nontaxable when made is not a promise that every later lapse is nontaxable.

Full surrender is not a death claim

Ordinary surrender income is the amount treated as received above the applicable investment in the contract. Income is ordinary, not converted into capital gain by a long holding period. The carrier generally reports the transaction on Form 1099-R. A personal policy loss does not automatically establish a deductible investment loss.

Example: With $55,000 proceeds, $40,000 adjusted basis and no debt or other effects, taxable gain is $15,000.

Death-benefit exclusion has exceptions

Section 101 generally excludes amounts paid because of the insured’s death. The transfer-for-value rule can limit the exclusion to consideration paid plus later premiums and specified amounts. There are statutory exceptions, including certain carryover-basis transfers and transfers to the insured, a partner, an eligible partnership or corporation; reportable policy sales can override those exceptions. Employer-owned life insurance has separate notice, consent and eligibility requirements.

Example: A sale to an unrelated investor is not interchangeable with an ordinary family beneficiary designation.

Calculate fixed installments separately from interest-only or life income

For a stated fixed number of death-benefit installments, divide the amount held at death by that number to determine the excluded portion under the applicable rule. Amounts above it are interest. A life-contingent option requires the appropriate life-expectancy and guarantee adjustment calculations. Buying a separate annuity after receiving a lump sum is a different tax transaction.

Example: A $200,000 benefit divided across 100 stated installments gives $2,000 excluded per installment; a $2,400 payment therefore includes $400 interest.

Gross estate is a different tax concept

Insurance payable to or for the estate and insurance subject to retained incidents of ownership can be included in the insured’s federal gross estate. Certain transfers within three years of death also matter. Inclusion is not itself a calculation of tax payable. Federal and Maryland estate rules are separate, and annual dollar thresholds must be dated when used.

Example: Naming an individual beneficiary does not eliminate the need to examine the insured’s retained ownership powers.
