01 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Three different kinds of permission

Elena holds an active Maryland life producer license. An insurer offers her a producer agreement. The state license, the business agreement and the insurer appointment are related but different. The license establishes permitted insurance authority in Maryland. The agreement sets working terms. The appointment documents the insurer relationship. The insurer must itself be authorized for the business. An enthusiastic email from a manager is not a reason to ignore the required documentation or make promises beyond delegated authority. Verify the role and the permission before acting.

02 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

The narrow life-application exception

Maryland permits a licensed producer, without an appointment, to make an informal life-insurance inquiry or solicit a life application for an insurer authorized for that business. This is a specific exception, not unrestricted authority to bind coverage. If an insurer initially accepts an application from an unappointed producer, it must, within thirty days, either reject the application under the applicable law or appoint the producer and enter the required information in its register. Acceptance for processing is not necessarily acceptance of the insurance risk or a promise that coverage has begun.

03 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Document and maintain the appointment

An insurer maintains its producer register and adds the required appointment information within thirty days. It also provides written appointment documentation within thirty days. The appointed producer keeps the documentation and a list of appointing insurers. With written documentation, the producer may act within authority before the insurer finishes the register entry. Appointment records stay available while the appointment is active and for at least five years after termination. Electronic records must be retrievable within ten working days after the Commissioner's request. A screenshot that cannot later be found is not a reliable record system.

04 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

When the relationship ends

An insurer updates its register within thirty days after the effective date of an appointment or other covered business-relationship termination. Certain terminations involving suspected grounds for discipline also require notice to the Commissioner. Within fifteen days after that required notice, the insurer sends a copy to the producer using the prescribed delivery method. The producer has thirty days after receiving the notice to submit comments, with a copy sent to the insurer. Ending one appointment does not by itself mean the state has revoked the person's license. Those are separate decisions.

05 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

The Commissioner enforces the law

The Maryland Insurance Commissioner enforces the Insurance Article and can conduct examinations and investigations within that authority. Grounds for producer discipline include misappropriating money, intentional material falsehoods in a license application, forging an insurance document, dishonest practices, and other specified violations. The statute provides notice and an opportunity for hearing for the stated licensing sanctions. Denial, suspension, revocation and refusal to renew have different effects. An insurer's appointment decision is not a substitute for the Commissioner's process, and a producer should not ignore a lawful order or request for records.

06 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Consequences and the right response

Under the producer discipline statute, the Commissioner may impose a penalty from one hundred to five thousand dollars per violation instead of, or in addition to, suspension or revocation. Restitution can also be required for financial injury. Other laws can create separate consequences. If an independent producer has good-faith cause to believe insurance fraud occurred, Maryland requires a written report to the Fraud Division. Do not conceal a suspected problem to protect a sale. Preserve the facts, use the required reporting process and cooperate within the law.

APPLY / IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Go one step deeper.

The register and written documentation are different

An appointed producer who has the written documentation may act before the insurer completes its register entry, within the scope of authority. The specific life-application exception is separate. Neither rule creates a general power to promise coverage before underwriting or receipt conditions are satisfied.

Example: Elena may submit a qualifying application under the exception; she may not tell the applicant that submission guarantees immediate coverage.

Not every termination notice has the same recipient or trigger

§10–118(e) requires register updates for covered terminations and regulator notice for the specified discipline-related reasons. §10–118(g) then gives a fifteen-day period for the insurer’s copy to the producer and thirty days for producer comments after receipt. Comments are simultaneously copied to the insurer.

Example: A routine business termination and a termination based on suspected dishonest activity should not be described as identical reporting events.

Hearings and judicial review

Under §2–210, a qualifying written hearing demand states the grounds. The Commissioner has thirty consecutive days to grant and hold the hearing, unless mutually postponed, or refuse it; failure to grant or refuse is deemed refusal. A petition for judicial review under §2–215 generally runs thirty days from the applicable order service or refusal event. Filing an appeal does not automatically establish a stay; the court determines that issue.

Example: A producer who disagrees with an order follows the stated review process rather than continuing prohibited conduct while assuming that a complaint suspends the order.

Recognize additional discipline grounds

§10–126 also addresses lack of trustworthiness or competence, specified convictions, failure to obey proper orders, certain tax failures, improper examination references and other enumerated conduct. Depending on the facts, misconduct by specified people can affect a business entity’s license as well.

Example: Forging a client’s signature to meet a deadline is a discipline issue even if the producer believes the client would have agreed.

Fraud reporting is based on good-faith cause

§27–802 directs the covered persons with good-faith cause to suspect insurance fraud to report it in writing. An independent producer specifically reports to the Fraud Division. The statute includes protections for good-faith reporting; these are not a license to make a knowingly false accusation.

Example: Document the reason for the concern and the relevant records, rather than treating a suspicion as a proven criminal conviction.
