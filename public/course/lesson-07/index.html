01 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Read the whole contract

Aisha and Marcus find a policy phrase they understand differently. Disagreement alone does not make language ambiguous. Read the words in their ordinary meaning and in the context of the whole policy. A genuine ambiguity can exist when language reasonably supports more than one meaning. Interpretation rules can protect reasonable expectations and resolve ambiguity, but do not assume a customer's hope overrides a clear exclusion. In Maryland, courts do not simply discard clear policy wording because one party expected broader protection.

02 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Representations and warranties

A representation is a statement offered as true to the best of the applicant's knowledge. A warranty is a stricter contractual assurance. Maryland law specifically treats statements by or for an insured or annuitant in a life or annuity application as representations, not warranties. That distinction does not excuse false information. Applicants should answer completely and accurately, and producers should follow the correction process when an error is found. Do not teach that every small error automatically voids a policy, or that calling an answer a representation makes it harmless.

03 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Why materiality matters

Under Maryland's rule, a misrepresentation, omission, concealment or incorrect statement can affect recovery if it is fraudulent or material to accepting the risk or the hazard assumed. The statute also addresses whether the insurer, knowing the correct facts, would in good faith have issued the coverage on the same terms. Intent to deceive is therefore not the only possible issue. This policy-application rule differs from the intentional-misrepresentation ground in a producer's license application. Identify which application and which rule the question actually concerns.

04 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Good faith, concealment and fraud

The doctrine of utmost good faith describes the importance of honest disclosure and dealing in insurance. Concealment involves withholding relevant information. Fraud involves intentional deception and its legal elements; it is not another name for every innocent mistake. Suppose an applicant deliberately hides a requested diagnosis to obtain a rate. The deliberate deception raises a different issue from an accidental typing error. Both should be corrected, but consequences depend on materiality, the statute, policy terms and other applicable limits, including contestability. A producer must never supply a false answer to obtain more favorable terms.

05 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Waiver and estoppel

Waiver is the voluntary relinquishment of a known right. Estoppel can prevent a party from taking a position inconsistent with conduct on which another person reasonably relied to their detriment. They are related but not identical. For example, knowingly choosing not to enforce a requirement raises a waiver question; misleading conduct followed by harmful reliance raises the second doctrine. Do not treat either label as a producer's power to create coverage, override law, or erase every exclusion. The available remedy depends on the facts and governing law.

Giving up a known right

Estoppel

Inconsistent conduct and detrimental reliance

06 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Choose the rule before the answer

Imagine Marcus notices an incorrect answer after submission. Aisha should promptly follow the insurer's correction procedure, not guess that the mistake is too small to matter. The insurer can evaluate the corrected facts. If a later dispute arises, distinguish an interpretation issue from a false statement, and distinguish a contractual right from an excluded risk. Your exam task is to identify the relevant facts and doctrine. Your professional task is to prevent avoidable disputes through accurate records and clear explanations from the start.

APPLY / IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Go one step deeper.

How Maryland resolves an ambiguity

Maryland first reads the contract as a whole using ordinary meaning. A genuine ambiguity can permit extrinsic evidence of the parties’ intent. If ambiguity remains after that evidence is considered, it ordinarily is resolved against the drafter; the court does not begin by automatically reading every clause against the insurer.

Example: A clearly stated benefit limit does not become ambiguous merely because a customer hoped for a larger payment.

Two Maryland application rules

Insurance §12–207 governs statements in applications for life/health insurance and annuities and includes materiality and specified good-faith underwriting tests. §10–126(a)(2) concerns intentional material misrepresentation or concealment in a producer-license application. The elements are not interchangeable.

Example: A question about an insured’s policy application cannot be answered solely by quoting the producer-license discipline standard.

Do not turn definitions into automatic legal outcomes

A basic definition identifies a doctrine, not the result of every dispute. Consider policy language, notice, authority, materiality, statutory restrictions and the facts. The course uses clearly specified hypotheticals to test the distinguishing concept.

Example: A party’s disappointment with a clear coverage limit does not itself establish ambiguity.
