01 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Field underwriting starts with accurate facts

Marcus is applying for a life policy. The producer asks the questions on the approved application, records his answers and gathers the requested supporting facts. This is field underwriting. It helps the insurer evaluate the risk, but it does not give the producer unlimited authority to approve coverage. The producer's report records relevant observations and transaction information truthfully. A blank answer is not automatically a no. An unfamiliar medication is not a diagnosis the agent should invent. Ask for clarification and follow the carrier's approved procedure. Explain that underwriting and any temporary protection depend on their actual requirements.

02 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Complete the record before signatures

Elena notices that the application lists the wrong medication date. Correct it through the insurer's approved process and obtain the required acknowledgment. Never erase a material answer secretly after signature or ask a client to sign a blank form for later completion. Verify the proposed insured, owner and other required signatures and each person's authority. When more than one person completes a Maryland application, the signature area must identify the portion to which each signature applies. A lawful electronic signature process can be used when accepted. Convenience does not authorize signing someone else's name.

03 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

A representation still has to be accurate

Maryland treats statements in a life application as representations, not warranties. Health questions must use the applicant's knowledge and belief standard or include the required representation statement. That does not make false answers harmless. Under Maryland law, a fraudulent or material misstatement can affect recovery, and the statute also considers whether correct facts would have changed the insurer's good-faith decision to issue, price or cover the risk. Do not teach that fraud is the only possible problem. An agent should record the truth and refer an uncertain answer for clarification, not coach the applicant to hide it.

04 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Notice and consent serve different purposes

The applicant should understand what information may be collected, how it may be used and the rights described in the applicable notice. Giving an information-practices notice is not the same as obtaining every required authorization. A consumer report containing medical information requires the applicant's permission before the reporting agency provides it. If underwriting calls for an HIV test, use the appropriate informed-consent and confidentiality process rather than treating an ordinary signature as permission for every test. Explain the current carrier forms. Collect only relevant information through authorized channels and protect it from unnecessary disclosure.

05 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Recognize unusual money movements

An applicant wants a large cash-value policy, seems unconcerned about protection and asks how quickly an unrelated person can receive a surrender payment. This combination calls for the insurer's anti-money-laundering review. The federal insurance rule focuses on covered products, including individual permanent life and individual annuities, with the stated cash-value and investment features. It does not impose an identical rule on every insurance product. The insurer must integrate relevant agents and brokers into its program. Follow training, identity and customer-information procedures, document the facts and escalate concerns. Do not promise confidentiality that would defeat a required report.

06 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Submit the file and explain what comes next

Before submission, check the plan, amount, riders, ownership, beneficiary information, answers, signatures, notices and any premium receipt. Tell Marcus what additional underwriting evidence may be needed. A submitted application is not the same as an issued policy or a promise of immediate coverage. If a consumer report contributes to an adverse decision, the required notice identifies the reporting agency and explains the consumer's report and dispute rights. The reporting agency did not make the insurer's decision. Help the applicant follow the review process while protecting privacy. Never solve a disappointing result by changing facts or hiding another application.

APPLY / IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Go one step deeper.

The actual application controls what must be answered

Read each question as written, including its time period and definitions. Obtain requested details and use an approved continuation form when needed. Do not substitute a general impression for a specific answer.

Example: If a question asks about consultations during the past five years, an agent should not silently change the period to one year.

Corrections remain visible to the proper parties

Use the insurer’s authorized amendment or correction process, with required applicant acknowledgment. The producer cannot turn an inaccurate signed application into an accurate one by secretly overwriting it.

Example: A changed medication date should be documented and accepted through the carrier’s process before the corrected record is relied upon.

Maryland’s misstatement rule is broader than intentional fraud alone

Insurance §12-207 addresses fraud, materiality and the insurer’s good-faith decision with the correct facts. A representation is not an absolute warranty, but a material incorrect statement can still matter. Apply all stated facts and other relevant policy rules.

Example: Do not answer that every innocent typo voids a policy, or that every unintentional material omission must be harmless.

HIV consent is a specific process

The linked Maryland carrier packet is an example, not a universal form for all insurers. It includes a separate HIV notice and consent form and explains information practices. Follow the actual insurer’s current approved forms and applicable consent rules.

Example: A general contact-information update is not a substitute for the required testing consent.

An agent participates in the insurer’s AML program

Under the cited insurance-company rule, an agent or broker does not need a separate program merely because of that role. Other authority, such as broker-dealer status, can create separate duties. The insurer’s program includes relevant agents and brokers and the insurer remains responsible.

Example: An unusual third-party payment or early surrender request is a reason to follow the program’s review process; it is not proof of a crime by itself.

Covered products have a defined scope

The rule includes individual permanent life, individual annuities and other insurance with cash-value or investment features. Ordinary term without those features and the specified group products are outside the cited covered-product definition. An insurer may still apply broader internal controls.

Example: Do not evade a company’s legitimate identification process by saying that a term product is outside this particular federal definition.
