01 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Start with the claim and the available choices

Elena is entitled to a payable life insurance benefit. A lump sum gives her the benefit in one payment. Other settlement options may spread proceeds over time, when the contract makes them available and any binding owner instructions permit the choice. A settlement payment option is different from selling a policy to an investor during the insured's lifetime. Maryland requires a policy offering installments or an annuity to state that feature and include determinable payment information. Begin with the actual contract, eligible payee and payment instructions. A textbook list of options does not mean every carrier offers every option on every policy.

02 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Interest-only payments preserve the stated principal

Assume an available interest-only arrangement leaves one hundred thousand dollars with the insurer and pays Elena the interest. At a hypothetical three percent annual rate, that principal generates three thousand dollars for a full year under a simple annual calculation. The payment uses interest rather than deliberately spending down principal. The contract governs withdrawal access, rate changes and what happens when Elena dies. Funds retained by an insurer are different from a bank deposit. Maryland permits the specified proceeds to remain among the insurer's general assets. Review the insurer's obligations and actual agreement instead of assuming federal bank-deposit insurance applies.

03 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Fixed period selects the length of time

The beneficiary wants the proceeds distributed over a stated number of years. Under a fixed-period option, the selected period determines the payment schedule, while the proceeds and applicable interest help determine each payment. Use a deliberately simplified example with no interest or fees: one hundred twenty thousand dollars divided into one hundred twenty monthly payments produces one thousand dollars per month for ten years. Real tables can include interest and other contract details. A fixed ten-year period is not a promise of income for Elena's entire lifetime. Read the remaining-payment provision if she dies before the selected period ends.

10 years = 120 monthly payments

04 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Fixed amount selects the payment size

Now Elena specifies two thousand dollars each month. Under a fixed-amount option, the chosen payment and the available proceeds with applicable interest determine how long payments continue. With one hundred twenty thousand dollars and no interest or fees in this teaching example, the money supplies sixty monthly payments, or five years. Choosing one thousand dollars instead would make the same assumed principal last twice as long. This is not automatically a lifetime guarantee. Compare it with fixed period by asking what Elena selected first: the monthly amount or the number of years. Read how actual credited interest affects the remaining schedule.

05 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Lifetime income depends on whose life is covered

A life-income option promises payments for the covered lifetime instead of merely until a chosen period or fund schedule ends. A straight single-life form stops when its measuring life dies, with no additional guarantee assumed. Life income with a period certain can add minimum remaining payments under its terms. Joint-and-survivor income uses two lives and continues the specified payment to the survivor. The survivor amount may stay the same or be reduced according to the selected option. For example, seventy-five percent of a stated two thousand dollar payment is fifteen hundred dollars. These are payment forms, not first-death versus second-death life policies.

06 / UNDERSTAND THE REASON

Interest before payment is a separate Maryland rule

Do not confuse electing interest-only income with interest owed on an unpaid death claim. Maryland generally measures interest from the insured's death until payment. An insurer need not pay that interest when proceeds are paid within thirty days after death. If proof of death arrives more than one hundred eighty days after death, the statutory interest begins when that proof is submitted. The rate cannot be lower than the insurer's rate on death proceeds left on deposit. These interest rules do not grant permission to delay a valid claim. Determine the claim dates first, then analyze any later settlement choice separately.

APPLY / IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Go one step deeper.

Choose only from rights actually available

A valid owner election can bind the method of payment. Otherwise the beneficiary chooses among available rights under the contract. Section 16-209 requires the installment or annuity provision and determinable payment table. Section 16-211 addresses proof of death, permitted surrender or interest evidence, and the refund of premiums paid after the month of death.

Example: A claim kit can limit settlement alternatives to certain contract issue years; this course teaches the forms without promising universal availability.

A stated interest-only rate is an assumption, not a quote

If principal is $200,000 and the explicitly assumed simple annual interest rate is 4%, a full year produces $8,000 before any stated adjustments. Principal withdrawal rights and the guaranteed or declared rate come from the contract. Interest earned can have different tax treatment from the death benefit itself; the later tax lessons address that distinction.

Example: Do not describe the hypothetical three-percent teaching example as an available carrier rate.

Proceeds held by an insurer have specific legal terms

Section 16-108 permits an insurer to hold proceeds under the policyholder’s written agreement or, absent that arrangement at maturity, an agreement with beneficiaries. Control, revocation and stated protections from beneficiary creditors follow that agreement and law. The funds need not be separately segregated.

Example: A limitation on beneficiary control is not an unlimited promise that all money is immune from every creditor or legal order.

Fixed period and fixed amount answer different questions

Under the expressly simplified no-interest, no-fee assumptions, $120,000 over ten years of monthly payments equals $1,000 per month. Selecting $2,000 per month instead gives sixty payments. Real settlement tables incorporate their applicable interest and terms.

Example: Choosing the period fixes duration; choosing the payment amount leaves the duration dependent on the available proceeds and applicable interest.

A lifetime promise differs from a minimum period

Straight life-only payments stop at the measuring life’s death. If an option instead promises life with ten years certain and the person dies after six complete years of monthly payments, the specified remaining four years are payable to the designated recipient under the guarantee. Living beyond ten years still produces life payments.

Example: A pure ten-year fixed-period option ends after ten years; life with ten years certain continues while the measuring life is alive.

Joint-and-survivor is an income form

The surviving covered person receives the continuation percentage or amount in the actual option. With an explicit 75% survivor feature applied to an initial $2,000 monthly payment, continuation is $1,500. A 100% feature instead preserves $2,000 under those assumptions.

Example: This is not the same as a joint first-death policy paying a death benefit or survivorship life paying at the last insured’s death.

Use the actual death-claim dates

Under §16-109, payment within thirty days after death receives the interest exception. Proof submitted more than 180 days after death shifts the interest start to the submitted-proof date. Otherwise the statutory general start is death. The minimum rate is tied to death proceeds left with the insurer.

Example: Death on January 1 with proof submitted after 180 days and payment later: use the actual proof-submission date as the interest start, not January 1.
